Confidence Ratings Explained

Every data point in a Vantage Point dossier carries one of four ratings. The rating is not decoration — it tells you exactly how much weight the finding can carry.

Rating definitions
RatingDefinitionHow It AppearsHow To Use It
Confirmed Independently corroborated by two or more qualifying sources. Stated as fact in findings. Safe to rely on for decision-making.
Corroborated Supported by one strong source; second source unavailable or inconclusive. Stated with explicit caveat. Usable, but verify before acting irreversibly.
Indicative Pattern or inference only; no direct documentary support. Flagged as a lead, never a finding. Direction for further work. Not evidence.
Unverified Could not be substantiated from any available source. Excluded from findings; disclosed as a gap. Do not act on it. Absence of record ≠ absence of fact.
Why We Publish Negative Findings

"We searched and found nothing" is a result worth paying for. Every dossier lists what we searched and came back empty on, so you can distinguish a clean record from an unsearched one. Absence of a public record is never presented as proof that an event did not occur.

Why We Publish Our Limitations

Every dossier includes a Method Limitations section stating what we could not reach and why — sealed records, jurisdictional gaps, probabilistic inference. An analyst who reports no limitations is either not looking hard enough or not telling you the truth.
